About these works

These are more than aquariums. They are living works of art, landscapes shaped by stone, water, plants, light, and time.

Their presence reflects a simple principle of biophilic design: bringing meaningful experiences of nature into the spaces where we learn, work, receive care, and connect.

Created in the tradition of Nature Aquarium, these works are never finished. Plants grow, water moves, and the landscape keeps changing with time.

The Creators

Who made these

Both are direct disciples of Takashi Amano, the photographer who invented Nature Aquarium. They learned at his side in Niigata and now carry the work forward.

The founder · 1954–2015

Takashi Amano

天野 尚

Photographer and founder of Aqua Design Amano in Niigata, Japan. He created the Nature Aquarium style: an aquarium that recreates a natural landscape as a living ecosystem of plants, stone, wood, fish and light. His last major work, Forests Underwater at the Oceanário de Lisboa, was 40 meters long and ran from 2015 to 2026. Learn more about Amano and ADA

Research

Why nature heals

Contact with living nature lowers stress and improves mood in many studies, including in hospitals. The evidence is promising but not final, which is why this gallery is a measured trial.

−4.8 mmHg

Systolic blood pressure fell about 4.8 mmHg when clinicians prescribed time in nature, with moderate drops in depression and anxiety.

28 studies · The Lancet Planetary Health, 2023
Large

Nature-based programs reduced depression by a large amount in people diagnosed with anxiety, depression or stress.

BMJ Open, 2025
= hypnosis

Watching an aquarium before oral surgery relaxed patients as much as hypnosis did, and more than looking at a poster.

42 patients · Katcher et al.
+17%

Residents with Alzheimer's ate 17% more and were less agitated after aquariums came to their units. A photo of the sea had no effect.

60 residents · Purdue University
More fish

Heart rate fell and mood rose while people watched an aquarium, and fuller tanks worked better.

National Marine Aquarium, Plymouth, 2016
15 min

Fifteen minutes of handling plants lowered diastolic blood pressure from 71.8 to 65.3 mmHg compared with computer work.

J. Physiological Anthropology, 2015
−10% stay

Surgical patients with a view of trees left hospital sooner and needed fewer strong painkillers than those facing a wall.

Ulrich, Science, 1984

Where studies disagree

A 2021 Zurich trial found that a tank in a waiting room did not change blood pressure or anxiety over 20 minutes, though patients much preferred the room with fish (PLOS ONE). That is why this gallery asks you to sit, look closely, and tell us what happened.
